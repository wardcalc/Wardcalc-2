Understanding the Wells Score for PE

Pulmonary Embolism (PE) is a potentially fatal condition that can present with vague or non-specific symptoms. The Wells Score allows clinicians to calculate a pre-test probability to determine the safest and most cost-effective diagnostic pathway.

Clinical Interpretations (Two-Tier Model)

  • PE Unlikely (≤ 4 points): Consider applying the PERC rule. If PERC positive or not applied, obtain a highly sensitive D-dimer test. A negative D-dimer rules out PE.
  • PE Likely (> 4 points): Proceed directly to diagnostic imaging, typically Computed Tomography Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA). A D-dimer is not recommended here, as a negative result does not safely rule out PE in a high-risk patient.

References: Wells PS, Anderson DR, Rodger M, et al. Derivation of a simple clinical model to categorize patients probability of pulmonary embolism. Thromb Haemost. 2000;83(3):416-420.
