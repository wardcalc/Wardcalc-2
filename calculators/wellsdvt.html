Understanding the Wells Score

The Wells Criteria for DVT was designed to stratify patients into risk categories for deep vein thrombosis, allowing clinicians to safely guide further diagnostic testing, such as D-dimer assays and venous ultrasonography.

Clinical Interpretations

  • High Risk (≥ 3 points): DVT is highly probable. Proceed directly to proximal leg ultrasound. If ultrasound is delayed, empirical anticoagulation may be indicated.
  • Moderate Risk (1-2 points): Obtain a high-sensitivity D-dimer test. If positive, proceed to ultrasound.
  • Low Risk (≤ 0 points): DVT is unlikely. A negative D-dimer effectively rules out DVT without the need for imaging.

References: Wells PS, Anderson DR, Bormanis J, et al. Value of assessment of pretest probability of deep-vein thrombosis in clinical management. Lancet. 1997;350(9094):1795-1798.
